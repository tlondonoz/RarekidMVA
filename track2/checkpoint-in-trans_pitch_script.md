### Slide 1 — 0:00–0:18
> Compound heterozygous *BUB1B*: one allele truncates before the pseudokinase domain, the other sits
> buried inside it. The lesion is dosage insufficiency of a scaffolding protein — and you cannot
> treat an insufficiency by inhibiting something. There is nothing to block.

### Slide 2 — 0:18–0:45
> So first, is BubR1 a target at all? No. Vertebrate BubR1 is a pseudokinase — catalytically dead,
> though essential as a scaffold — and the missense sits thirty-five ångström from the nucleotide
> site. Open Targets also reports no druggable pocket, and we deliberately do **not** use that: it
> needs an experimental structure, and none covers this domain. That is absence of evaluation, not
> a negative.

### Slide 3 — 0:45–1:05
> And what we would actually do first is not a drug. MVA predisposes to Wilms tumour, including
> bilateral. This child is already under renal follow-up for nephrocalcinosis, so serial renal
> ultrasound costs almost nothing and risks nothing. It has a better benefit-to-risk ratio than
> anything else in our report, so we put it ahead of all three drug proposals.

### Slide 4 — 1:05–1:38
> Our lead drug proposal is mTOR inhibition, because two independent justifications converge on it.
> Temsirolimus has randomised trial data in rhabdomyosarcoma — his exact histology, his age group,
> added to chemotherapy. And mTORC1 is hyperactive in BubR1-mutant mice, his genotype. No other
> candidate has both.
> The honest objection is ours too: he has muscle atrophy, short stature and failure to thrive, and
> mTORC1 is the central regulator of growth. The mouse rationale and the clinical reality point in
> opposite directions on the same tissue. We state that; we do not resolve it.

### Slide 5 — 1:38–2:20
> Now the analysis that changed our own ranking. Readthrough looked ideal — the context is the most
> permissive stop class. A class-level recommendation stops there.
> We asked one more question: readthrough does not restore the original amino acid. At TGA it
> installs tryptophan, cysteine or arginine. Does the protein tolerate them at position 737?
> Across two hundred and forty-seven vertebrate orthologs, leucine in two hundred and thirty-six, and
> none of the three anywhere. But compare them against what this position *does* tolerate: serine is
> a bigger cavity than cysteine. So only tryptophan truly exceeds the range — and we report that
> against our own argument. Readthrough went from first to third.

### Slide 6 — 2:20–3:00
> Finally, what we argue against. Aneuploid tumours depend on their residual checkpoint, so
> checkpoint inhibitors are the standard play. Not here: that window exists because normal cells have
> an intact checkpoint, and this child's do not. Perturbing it could raise aneuploidy in healthy
> tissue — his tumour predisposition mechanism.
> We packaged the triage as a tool for any nonsense variant, any recessive disease. And to be clear:
> these are hypotheses for laboratory follow-up in patient-derived cells. The next step is a dish,
> not a prescription.
