# Track 2 pitch — 3 minutes
**Team checkpoint-in-trans · Proband PROBAND01**

Read at ~150 words/minute. Spoken text is the quoted lines only.

---
### Slide 1 — 0:00–0:18
> This child has compound heterozygous *BUB1B*. One allele truncates before the pseudokinase domain;
> the other sits buried inside it. The lesion is dosage insufficiency of a scaffolding protein — and
> you cannot treat an insufficiency by inhibiting something. There is nothing to block.

### Slide 2 — 0:18–0:50
> So first we asked whether BubR1 is a target at all. Three independent lines say no. Thirteen of
> sixteen papers that take a position call vertebrate BubR1 a pseudokinase — catalytically dead,
> though still essential as a scaffold. Structurally, the missense sits thirty-five ångström from the
> nucleotide site. And Open Targets evaluates twenty-eight tractability buckets for this gene: no
> pocket of high or medium quality, no clinical compound.
> That is why we docked nothing. There is no pocket, and the direction we need is restoration.

### Slide 3 — 0:50–1:30
> Our lead candidate comes from a mouse. BubR1 insufficiency is the founding model of the senolytics
> field — and one strain is closer still: BubR1-H over L1002P combines a reduced-protein allele with
> a missense in the same C-lobe. That is this patient's allelic architecture. Those mice show the
> full MVA phenotype, and the severity of their progeroid pathology tracked with the complexity of
> the senescence secretory phenotype.
> So: clear the senescent cells. Dasatinib is FDA-approved, pediatric from age one, and one
> indication is in combination with chemotherapy. The dosing in a child on cytotoxic therapy exists.

### Slide 4 — 1:30–2:15
> The obvious candidate was readthrough — the truncating allele is a nonsense variant. The context is
> favourable: TGA with adenine at plus four, the most permissive stop class.
> A class-level recommendation stops there. We asked one more question. Readthrough does not restore
> the original amino acid; at TGA it inserts tryptophan, cysteine or arginine. Does BubR1 tolerate
> any of them at position 737?
> We read that position across two hundred and forty-seven vertebrate orthologs. Leucine in two
> hundred and thirty-six. Tryptophan, cysteine, arginine: zero — none, in any vertebrate. And the
> position is ninety percent buried.
> We demoted our own favourite candidate to third.

### Slide 5 — 2:15–2:38
> One finding a clinical team needs. Aneuploid tumours depend on residual checkpoint, so checkpoint
> inhibitors are the standard play. Not here. That window exists because normal cells have an intact
> checkpoint — this child's do not. It may be absent, or inverted.
> On spindle poisons the evidence is split, thirty papers against eighteen. Nothing justifies
> touching his chemotherapy.

### Slide 6 — 2:38–3:00
> We packaged the triage as a tool: any nonsense variant in, and out comes the stop class, the
> context, NMD competence, and whether the protein tolerates what readthrough would insert. Thousands
> of recessive diseases, not one child.
> And to be clear: these are hypotheses for laboratory follow-up in patient-derived cells. The next
> step is a dish, not a prescription.
