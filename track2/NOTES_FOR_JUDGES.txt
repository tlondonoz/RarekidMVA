Notes for judges — checkpoint-in-trans, Track 2

Three things worth knowing before you open the report.

1. We rank a NON-DRUG intervention first. Serial renal ultrasound for Wilms tumour has a better
   benefit-to-risk ratio than any of our three drug proposals, and the child is already under renal
   follow-up for nephrocalcinosis, so the incremental cost is near zero. We put it ahead of the
   pharmacology deliberately rather than omit it.

2. We argue explicitly AGAINST a strategy the literature would otherwise suggest. Aneuploid tumours
   depend on their residual spindle checkpoint, so KIF18A and related inhibitors are the standard
   play. That window assumes normal cells keep an intact checkpoint — which is false in constitutional
   BUB1B insufficiency, where perturbing the residual checkpoint could raise aneuploidy in healthy
   tissue and so increase tumour risk. Section 8.

3. Our methodological contribution produced a NEGATIVE result about our own favourite candidate.
   Readthrough does not restore the wild-type residue; at UGA it inserts Trp, Cys or Arg. We asked
   whether BubR1 tolerates the product at position 737 across 247 vertebrate orthologs, and the answer
   demoted translational readthrough from first to third. The same analysis also shows the exclusion
   is not uniform — only Trp exceeds the volume change this position tolerates — and we report that
   against our own interest. The triage ships as ptc_triage.py and generalises to any nonsense allele.

The report separates "not evaluated" from "negative" throughout; where we could not measure something
we say so rather than infer it. The repository carries the code, the 423 structured literature
extractions, and a data-availability statement explaining why no patient sequence data is included.

Nothing in the submission is a treatment recommendation.
